You can write "in partnership with Traditional Owners" into a contract and still run the project exactly as you would have without them. The difference shows up in the mechanics, not the wording — who sets the scope, and when.

On the Torres Shire Horn Island survey, the four sites we covered and the eleven flight routes it took — flown with a DJI Matrice 4T, a drone built for large-area mapping work — were worked out with Traditional Owners before the quote was finalised, not slotted in afterward around a budget that was already locked.

That's the actual test I'd give any contract claiming partnership: was the scope built with Traditional Owners, or just reviewed by them? One shapes what gets surveyed and how. The other just gets a sign-off on a plan someone else already wrote.

It's a small distinction on paper and a large one in practice — it's the difference between a genuine say in the work and a courtesy.

How do we use the world's newest technology while walking in the world's oldest living culture? On a contract, it starts with who's in the room when the scope gets written, not just who signs it.
