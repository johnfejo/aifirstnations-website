The first version of any field app anyone pitches me assumes you'll have signal. At Goondoi, you mostly don't.

That's not a minor technical detail — it's the difference between a tool rangers actually use and one that sits unused on a shelf. The Ranger Field App is built offline-first, which means it doesn't need a live connection to log an observation; it works exactly the same whether there are five bars or none, and syncs whatever it recorded once a signal turns up again. Eighteen observation categories, captured in the bush, no waiting for a data connection that might not show up until the ute's back on the highway.

Most software gets designed around the conditions a developer works in — good wifi, a desk, a charger nearby. Fieldwork on Country is the opposite of that. The lesson is simple but easy to skip: build for the actual conditions of the work, not the convenient ones, or the tool won't get used at all.

How do we use the world's newest technology while walking in the world's oldest living culture? Sometimes it's just making sure the technology still works when the signal doesn't.
