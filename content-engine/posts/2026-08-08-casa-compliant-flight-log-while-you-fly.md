As a Chief Remote Pilot under my own ReOC, flight logs aren't optional paperwork for me — they're a legal requirement, and getting them wrong isn't a small mistake.

The old way was: fly, then sit down later and reconstruct times, locations, and conditions from memory or scattered notes. That's slow, and it's exactly the kind of task where details get lost. The Ranger Field App builds the CASA-compliant flight log automatically, as the flight happens — the record exists the moment the work does, not hours later when the details have gone soft.

That matters beyond convenience. A compliance record built from memory is a weaker record, even if nothing was done wrong — it's harder to trust and slower to produce if it's ever needed. A record built in real time is simply more accurate, because there's no gap between the flying and the logging for anything to get lost in.

This is the kind of AI use I actually stand behind: not replacing the pilot's responsibility, just making sure the evidence of doing the job properly exists without extra effort.

How do we use the world's newest technology while walking in the world's oldest living culture? Here, it's as practical as a logbook that keeps up with you.
