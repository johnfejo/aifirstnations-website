I've sat in enough project meetings to know what a checkbox version of community engagement looks like: a single consultation session, minutes taken, a line added to the report, and the plan underneath barely moves.

Goondoi didn't work like that. The pond apple treatment plan — the method, the site boundaries, the timing — came out of an ongoing relationship with the Rangers and Traditional Owners on that Country, not a single meeting bolted onto an already-finished plan. Stem injection, treating 500-plus trees a session with a herbicide chosen specifically for that method, was agreed as the right approach for that place, not applied because it's the default.

The difference isn't really about how many meetings happen. It's about whether engagement can still change the plan, or whether it's just being told about one. If a project's scope, method, and timing are locked before Traditional Owners are in the room, no amount of consultation afterward turns that into a partnership.

How do we use the world's newest technology while walking in the world's oldest living culture? Ask whether the plan could still change after the conversation — that's the honest test.
