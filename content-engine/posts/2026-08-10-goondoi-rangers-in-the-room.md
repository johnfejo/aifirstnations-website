Before a drone goes up at Goondoi, Rangers are there — not signing off from an office beforehand, but on-site, at the time of the flight.

That's a different kind of governance than the upfront agreement that sets a project's scope. This is about who's present in the moment the work actually happens: what gets flown over, what doesn't, and whether something on the day needs the plan adjusted. A site can look different from how it did during initial planning — conditions change, something culturally significant might need a wider buffer than anticipated, and that's a call best made by people who know the Country, standing on it, not by a plan written weeks earlier.

It's a small operational detail with a real effect: decisions that would otherwise wait for a follow-up conversation get made correctly the first time, on the spot.

Cultural governance isn't only about consultation before a project starts. Sometimes the more important version of it is who's standing next to the pilot when the drone actually takes off.

How do we use the world's newest technology while walking in the world's oldest living culture? At Goondoi, it's Rangers on-site, not just Rangers consulted.
